```

There would be a 2-levels of analysis for our target population that we would want to look at: 
    1. A representative sample for each generation (i.e. across all people alive today)
    2. A representative sample of the same people in different generations.

For individuals not likely to be in the database, such as older adults, we would recruit from nearby senior homes, facebook pages. 

Sampling Units: Our sampling units would be each person with an account. 

Observational Units: Each person taking the test. It would be administered online.

## Part B - Survey Evaluation:

Identify and describe survey features:

```
1. Sample type
    Stratefied two-phase sampling since data from two different surveys was linked. 
    
    The first phase is based on the long form questionaire (randomly selected 1/4 dwellings in Canada). This is based on stratefied systemic sampling. First the sample is divided based on geographical zone to ensure that each area is represented. Then each household is ordered and every 4th dwelling is selected to recieve the long-form questionaire.

    The second phase looks at individuals, and it used rejective sampling. Rejective sampling works by rejecting a certain portion of the population with a predetermined propbability to allow for more time and effort to find the population of interest. For example, if we want to do a study comparing equal number of males and females, and we were finding that more males were volunteering to do the study, we might reject males after a certain point to ensure that we get enough females in the study. 

2. Sample size
        The sample size is 80,000 individuals (60,000 regular sample, 20,000 oversample). 

3. Target population
    All person 15 years of age or older in Canada, excluding residents of Yukon, Northwest Territories, Nunavut, full-time residents of institutions, and residents of First Nations reserves.

4. Sampling frame
    The survey frame is based on a list of all operational residential dwellings in Canada.  

5. Survey mode(s)
    Data was collected with electronic questionaire (EQ) or computer assisted telephone interviewing (CATI). The survey was available in the participant's official language of choice. 

6. Timeline
    Survey collected every 5 years. Collected from from 2023-09-15 to 2024-03-30.

7. Response rate
    The overall response rate was 40.9% (42.2% for regular sample and 37.1% for oversample).

8. Weights
    The weights were based on several factors. The first is that every person selected in to the census represented more than themselves in the sample. To adjust for the number of individuals that were not volunteers, the participants that were not volunteers were multipled by a fraction to reduce their weighting and the number of participants that were volunteer were multiplied by a whole number to increase their weighting. The final weight adjustment was for income distribution so that it would match the Canadian Income Survey distributed for each province.

9. Data processing
    Data was collected in census through an online survey or over the phone. These sources of data were then inputted into a database online. They then did the same for the second survey about volunteering.  

10. Cleaning, imputation, etc
    Data was input by the partcipants of the study. This means that the participant reads throug hthe question and then is inputting the records through a score. 
    There were three main validation measures used: 
    1. Analysis of changes over time
        
    2. Verification of estimates through cross-tabulations
    3. Confrontation with other similar sources of data. 
        This means that they compared this dat with other data sources to make sure that they were in line. 


11. Sources of error
    Common sources of error are imperfect coverage and non-response. Imperfect coverage is largely due to that people without good contact information were excluded, and thus this might introduce bias. Non-response is also an issue whereby individuals choose not to respond, which may be due to a third, unaccounted for variable. This would have an effet on our sampling and might introudce a bias that we are not accounting for since we cannot know about the individuals who did not fill out the survey. 

12. Limitations, known biases, etc
    The sourecs of error above would likely introduce biases. Especially with issues of non-responding, or if the definitions of volunteering is not the same for all participants and they self-select out.
